# Example: Audit report and discussion handoff

This abbreviated fictional example demonstrates the stable report produced by `$audit-x-account`. A real report includes the complete evidence coverage and scorecard.

## X account audit: @example

**Audit date:** 2026-08-14

**Objective:** Qualified audience growth among first-time SaaS founders

**Evidence window:** 18 public original posts published over 31 days; no owner analytics supplied

**Account stage:** Learning — the account has usable history but no repeated audience-aligned pattern

**Audit score:** Not scored — the public sample is sufficient for diagnosis but lacks comparable impression data

**Confidence:** Medium

### Executive verdict

Recent posts address several unrelated audiences, so a relevant stranger cannot predict what following will deliver. The next decision is to choose one audience promise and test it across a controlled set of useful originals before increasing cadence.

### Evidence coverage

| Evidence | Window or source | Observation | Label |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Profile | Captured 2026-08-14 | Bio promises general “AI and business” insight | Observed |
| Post sample | 18 originals over 31 days | Seven unrelated subject clusters | Observed |
| Public metrics | Mixed observation windows | Counts are not safely comparable | Observed / limitation |
| Owner analytics | Not supplied | Impressions, bookmarks, profile visits, and follows unknown | Unknown |

### F1 — Unclear audience promise

- **Evidence:** the bio and recent originals move between unrelated creator, consumer, and company audiences.
- **Interpretation:** the public sample provides too little coherent inventory for one intended audience.
- **Source boundary:** Observed plus Inference.
- **Layer:** Retrieval and Conversion.
- **Limitation:** the audit cannot reproduce X's internal topic assignments or viewer candidate sets.

### Primary constraint

> The primary constraint is an inconsistent audience promise, which makes both content matching and the human decision to follow less predictable for first-time SaaS founders.

### Prioritised action points

| ID | Action | Evidence addressed | Why now | Time horizon | Success signal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A1 | Rewrite the profile around one recurring promise for first-time SaaS founders | F1 | Every later test depends on a stable audience | Today | A relevant stranger can state what the account repeatedly provides |
| A2 | Publish six matched posts across two connected pillars without changing audience or proof standard | F1 | Creates the first comparable learning set | Next 14 days | Two executions attract substantive replies from the intended audience |
| A3 | Record each post on the same 48-hour window and classify the result `Retain`, `Revise`, `Stop`, or `Unresolved` | Missing comparable metrics | Prevents an outlier from setting strategy | Review on day 16 | A documented decision exists for each tested mechanism |

### Recommended stage handoff

Use `$learn-x-account` to design A2 and A3 as a controlled cycle. The audit owns this diagnosis; the Learning skill owns the detailed posts, experiment cards, and decision rules.

### Discuss this report

We can now work through A1, A2, or A3; challenge the Learning classification; or add owner analytics without rerunning the whole audit.

For example, a follow-up saying `Let's work through A1` should expand the profile decision only. It should not reproduce the evidence table or create a second audit report.
