# Example: Learning-cycle pack

This abbreviated example shows the expected shape of a `$learn-x-account` result. The account and metrics are fictional.

## Input

- 24 recent original posts measured after 48 hours
- Audience: solo consultants building productised services
- Pillars: positioning, sales calls, delivery systems
- Median impressions: 620
- Median qualified replies: 2
- Owner reports that positioning posts attract the right people but results vary sharply

## Stage fit

**Learning — medium confidence.** There is enough comparable history to test, but no mechanism has repeated often enough to become a dependable series.

## Baseline excerpt

| Pattern | Runs | Median impressions | Median qualified replies | Interpretation |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Abstract positioning advice | 6 | 430 | 1 | Weak proof and low specificity |
| Annotated offer examples | 4 | 1,020 | 5 | Promising, but examples and format changed together |
| Sales-call observations | 5 | 710 | 3 | Audience aligned; openings are inconsistent |

These are account-level audit metrics, not an internal X score.

## Primary uncertainty

Do annotated examples outperform plain text because they contain concrete proof, or because the image presentation earns more attention?

## Experiment card

- Hypothesis: a real before/after offer example will improve qualified replies relative to abstract advice.
- Control: same audience, pillar, problem, posting range, and natural action.
- Changed variable: concrete before/after evidence versus abstract explanation.
- Format control: test both versions as text first; test presentation separately later.
- Success evidence: repeated improvement over the relevant median plus replies from the intended audience.
- Guardrail: no invented client details; anonymise real material with permission.
- Minimum useful sample: three credible executions per variant.
- Decision date: after all six posts have a comparable 48-hour window.

## Six-post matched set

| Pair | Control | Variant |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 1 | Three signs an offer is too broad | An anonymised broad offer rewritten around one buyer and result |
| 2 | Why deliverables hide value | Before/after scope showing the decision a buyer can now make |
| 3 | How to make a sales call specific | A real question sequence and the insight each answer unlocked |

Each draft must use creator-confirmed examples. Media is excluded from this cycle so evidence specificity is the main changed variable.

## Decision rule

- **Retain:** the evidence variant beats the relevant median in at least two of three executions and improves audience quality.
- **Revise:** attention improves but qualified conversation does not.
- **Stop:** the variant repeatedly misses despite credible examples and execution.
- **Unresolved:** windows, samples, or audience evidence are not comparable.

One winning execution remains a candidate, not a proven pattern.
