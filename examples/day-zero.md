# Example: Day-zero launch pack

This abbreviated example shows the expected shape of a `$launch-x-account` result. The account is fictional; numbers are measurement fields, not forecasts.

## Input

- Creator: product designer who has shipped onboarding work for small SaaS teams
- Audience: first-time SaaS founders doing their own onboarding
- Goal: earn a qualified audience for a free onboarding teardown library
- Capacity: four feed posts and three useful conversation sessions per week
- Starting evidence: no posts and no relevant followers

## Stage fit

**Day zero — high confidence.** There is no relevant post history to score and no repeated performance pattern. The immediate job is to create legible, useful discovery inventory and a comparable evidence base.

## Positioning spine

- Audience: first-time SaaS founders with an activated product but weak onboarding
- Recurring problem: users sign up but do not reach first value
- Promise: practical onboarding teardowns founders can apply without a design team
- Proof: shipped onboarding work, annotated before/after screens, and public reasoning
- Boundary: no general startup motivation, fundraising commentary, or unsupported growth claims

## Profile pack

- Display name: `Alex Chen · SaaS onboarding`
- Bio: `I break down SaaS onboarding so first-time founders can get users to value faster. Product designer. New teardown every week ↓`
- Banner direction: one annotated activation journey with the line `Make first value obvious.`
- Pinned post opening: `Most onboarding advice starts after the first mistake: asking users to learn your product before they feel its value.`
- Link action: `Browse the teardown library`

Profile elements are human conversion surfaces. This example does not claim the banner has a direct Home-ranking coefficient.

## Three pillars

1. **Teardowns:** one screen, one friction point, one practical fix.
2. **Principles:** reusable rules for activation, empty states, and first-run choices.
3. **Build notes:** what the creator tried, what changed, and what remains uncertain.

## First-five excerpt from the 15-post matrix

| # | Pillar | Job | Opening | Proof needed | Media |
| ---: | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Teardowns | Establish utility | `This empty state gives a new user three jobs before one useful result.` | Annotated, permission-safe screen | Single image with numbered callouts and alt text |
| 2 | Principles | Create a memorable rule | `Good onboarding removes a decision before it adds an explanation.` | Two contrasting interface examples | Side-by-side crop if readable |
| 3 | Build notes | Show credible process | `I rewrote this activation checklist after watching where users paused.` | Creator's real observations | Text unless the checklist adds proof |
| 4 | Teardowns | Invite expert disagreement | `The problem with this welcome modal is not the copy. It is the timing.` | Annotated sequence | Three-frame image |
| 5 | Principles | Earn saves | `Before adding another tooltip, answer these four questions.` | Creator-reviewed checklist | Text or accessible checklist card |

The remaining ten briefs would keep five posts per pillar while varying evidence, angle, and format. No proof placeholder may be published as though it were a real result.

## Operating loop

- Publish four sustainable originals per week; this cadence is a capacity choice, not an algorithm rule.
- Join relevant conversations in three bounded sessions with specific, non-repetitive replies.
- Record each post after publication and compare a consistent 48-hour window where practical.
- Review after the first 15 classifiable posts.

## Exit gate

Move to Learning when 15 posts can be compared by pillar, opening, proof, format, and audience response. Do not graduate because of one outlier or a follower threshold.
