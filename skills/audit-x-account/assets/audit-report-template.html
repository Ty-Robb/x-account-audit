04 · Findings

The evidence behind the recommendation

Three findings only—the ones that materially change the plan.

F1 · CONVERSION Observed

The profile names a useful audience but does not finish the promise.

Evidence: the bio states the experience, work and audience, while the banner carries no readable proposition and the link points to a general GitHub profile.

Interpretation: a qualified visitor must discover the actual tool or proof unaided.

Limitation: profile visits and follow conversion were not supplied.

F2 · RETRIEVAL + HUMAN EXPECTATION Observed + inference

The recent public sample does not repeat one audience promise.

Evidence: the visible sample alternates between source-code analysis, a Crocs service complaint, creative-challenge posts, and a reply.

Interpretation: both machine topic matching and the human follow decision receive a less predictable signal than a connected three-pillar system would provide.

Limitation: five posts cannot establish the composition of the complete recent timeline.

F3 · PRODUCTION Practice

The strongest strategic asset is currently a one-off, not a repeatable format.

Evidence: the article about growing from zero connects source evidence, a specific audience problem, and a reusable open-source project.

Interpretation: turning the same proof into teardowns, build notes and experiments can create more discovery inventory without changing the core promise.

Limitation: public views alone do not show saves, qualified follows, or downstream repository use.

Primary constraint: the profile promise and recent visible content do not yet form one repeatable route from useful discovery to a credible next step for founders and lean teams.
