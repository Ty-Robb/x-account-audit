# Audit report template

Use this template only in Audit report mode. Produce a stable, self-contained report that the user can discuss section by section after delivery.

## Contents

1. Report identity and executive verdict
2. Evidence coverage and confidence
3. Stage and scorecard
4. Findings and primary constraint
5. Account cleanup and content direction
6. First-sprint action plan and execution board
7. Stage handoff and measurement
8. Open questions and follow-up
9. Optional appendices and methodology

## X account audit: @handle

**Audit date:** [ISO date]

**Objective:** [qualified followers, authority, leads, community, or stated objective]

**Evidence window:** [dates, number of posts, public or owner-supplied sources]

**Account stage:** [Day zero, Learning, Repeatability, or Compounding] — [brief evidence]

**Audit score:** [number]/100 — [result band]

**Confidence:** [High, Medium, or Low]

If evidence is insufficient, replace the score with `Not scored — insufficient public evidence`. Never award points for missing information.

### Executive verdict

State, in this order:

1. the single biggest constraint;
2. its practical consequence;
3. the most important decision the account should make next.

Keep this to one short paragraph. The user should understand the report before reading the detail.

### Evidence coverage

| Evidence | Window or source | Observation | Label |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Profile | Captured [date] | ... | Observed |
| Post sample | [count and dates] | ... | Observed |
| Public metrics | [window] | ... | Observed / Unknown |
| Owner analytics | [window or not supplied] | ... | Owner-supplied / Unknown |
| Under the Hood | [period, not supplied, or not eligible] | ... | Owner-supplied / Unknown |

List material access limitations immediately after the table. Do not let missing evidence disappear inside later caveats.

### Stage finding

Explain why the account is in the selected stage using repeatability, audience quality, conversion, and production evidence. Follower count alone is not a stage test.

State the evidence needed to increase confidence or change the classification.

### Scorecard

| Dimension | Score | Evidence-led explanation |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Profile conversion | /15 | ... |
| Topic coherence | /20 | ... |
| Discovery inventory | /20 | ... |
| Predicted-action potential | /25 | ... |
| Semantic variety | /10 | ... |
| Trust and negative-feedback risk | /10 | ... |

When the account cannot be scored, use `Strong`, `Mixed`, `Weak`, or `Unknown` and omit the total.

### Findings

Give only findings that materially affect the objective. For each finding, use:

#### F[number] — [short finding name]

- **Evidence:** the public or supplied observation.
- **Interpretation:** what the evidence likely means for this account.
- **Source boundary:** `Observed`, `Code-backed`, `Inference`, `Practice`, or `Unknown`.
- **Layer:** `Eligibility`, `Retrieval`, `Ranking`, `Slate`, `Visibility`, `Conversion`, or `Production` when relevant.
- **Limitation:** the most important fact the audit cannot establish.

Use no more than five code-backed findings. Published coefficients are evidence about one released path, not points awarded to observed actions.

### Primary constraint

Write one sentence:

> The primary constraint is [specific constraint], which causes [observable consequence] for [intended audience or objective].

Do not list several equal “main” problems.

### Account cleanup register

Include this register whenever the audit recommends changing a profile surface, recurring behaviour, destination, or visible post pattern. Omit irrelevant rows; do not pad the section.

| Target | Decision | Exact implementation | Why | Done when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [bio, banner, link, pin, series, post URL, behaviour] | Keep / Change / Reframe / Stop | [replacement copy, destination, treatment, or precise instruction] | [finding addressed] | [observable acceptance check] |

Use `Reframe` when the underlying activity can support the account promise after adding context, proof, or a clearer audience lesson. Use `Stop` for a future pattern that should not continue. Do not advise mass deletion merely to make the timeline look coherent. If a specific post should be deleted, name it and give the concrete trust, safety, accuracy, legal, or deliberate brand reason; deletion remains the owner's decision.

When profile changes are recommended, supply usable replacement copy or a fill-in-the-blank draft. Do not leave the owner with `improve the bio`, `clarify the banner`, or `add a better CTA`.

### Content direction

Include this section whenever topic, format, proof, or production is part of the primary constraint or an action. Define no more than three connected pillars.

| Pillar | Audience problem and recurring promise | Post types to publish | Proof to use | Formats | Exclude or reframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ... | ... | ... | ... | ... | ... |

Add a **starting queue** of four to seven specific first-sprint concepts covering the recommended pillars. Each concept must identify the angle and proof or example it needs; these are briefs, not automatically full drafts. Name the intended content mix or test structure when it is decision-relevant, and label it as a practice or experiment rather than an algorithm rule.

### Action plan

Use stable IDs so follow-up discussion can refer to them without recreating the report.

Begin with the first physical action:

> **Start here:** [one task] — **Owner:** [Agent / Owner / Joint] — **Time:** [estimate]

Then summarise the three outcomes:

| ID | Outcome | Evidence addressed | Why now | Time horizon |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A1 | [specific result under the owner's control] | [F# or observation] | [priority logic] | [date or bounded period] |
| A2 | ... | ... | ... | ... |
| A3 | ... | ... | ... | ... |

### Execution board

Keep the first sprint to the smallest useful sequence, normally no more than seven tasks or seven days.

| When | Action | Exact task | Owner | Status | Deliverable | Effort | Done when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Now | A1 | [next physical action] | Agent / Owner / Joint | Done / Ready / Needs input / Blocked | [actual output or named artifact] | [estimate] | [acceptance check] |
| Next | A2 | ... | ... | ... | ... | ... | ... |
| Later only if [trigger] | A3 | ... | ... | ... | ... | ... | ... |

Use `Agent` when the agent can create the deliverable now, `Owner` for live X-account actions, and `Joint` for a decision or review requiring both. Mark an agent-created artifact `Done` when it is included in the report. Use `Needs input` only when one specific missing fact prevents credible execution; name that fact. Do not create a large backlog from minor observations.

### Ready-to-use outputs

Place the artifacts required by the execution board here. Include only what the audit actually recommends, such as:

- exact bio, banner, link, pinned-post, or profile-destination copy;
- a cleanup checklist with named targets and dispositions;
- four to seven first-sprint content briefs covering the recommended pillars;
- an experiment card with control, variable, sample, window, success evidence, and decision rule;
- a copyable measurement-ledger header;
- a creative brief for any recommended image or video.

Each content brief must state the audience problem, post type, angle or opening, proof required, format, natural reader action, and measurement date. Draft the full post when the user requested drafts; otherwise make the brief specific enough that drafting is mechanical.

If an output can be created from available evidence, create it. Do not write `the user should draft...`. If a claim, result, asset, or preference is missing, provide a labelled fill-in-the-blank and place only that decision in `Needs input`.

### Later only if

List only conditional actions that should wait for evidence. State the trigger. Examples: create a named series only after a pattern repeats, increase cadence only after quality is sustainable, or expand topics only after the core audience responds.

Exactly three action outcomes are the default. Do not use `post consistently`, `engage more`, `improve quality`, `fix the profile`, `find your niche`, or another vague phrase without producing the task, output, owner, and completion check.

### Recommended stage handoff

Name the matching workflow and explain what it should execute:

- `$launch-x-account` for Day zero;
- `$learn-x-account` for Learning;
- `$systemize-x-account` for Repeatability;
- `$compound-x-account` for Compounding.

The audit report owns the diagnosis, cleanup decisions, content direction, ready-to-use outputs, and executable first sprint. The stage skill owns the extended post cycle, additional drafts, production system, or compounding programme.

### Measurement and review

Define:

- the smallest comparable evidence set needed next;
- a consistent observation window where practical;
- the primary outcome and necessary guardrails;
- the review date;
- the stage exit or regression condition.

Track only metrics available from public evidence or explicitly supplied by the owner. Profile visits matter to human conversion but are not proof of a direct ranking benefit.

### Open questions

List only missing evidence that could materially change the diagnosis, action order, or stage. Do not turn optional analytics into a barrier to using the report.

### Discuss this report

End with a concise invitation such as:

> We can now work through A1, A2, or A3; challenge the stage classification; or add new evidence without rerunning the whole audit.

In subsequent responses, refer to the report date, finding IDs, and action IDs. Create an addendum when new evidence changes a material conclusion. Do not silently rewrite the original report.

## HTML report rendering

Use `../assets/audit-report-template.html` when the user requests HTML, a visual report, a browser preview, or a shareable report file. Treat the HTML as a view of this canonical report, not a separate output contract.

1. Copy the asset to the authorised destination; do not edit the bundled template in place.
2. Replace every worked-example value. Never carry the example handle, counts, findings, copy, URLs, content briefs, dates, or actions into another account's report.
3. Populate the same nine report blocks: verdict, evidence, assessment, findings, cleanup, content direction, action plan, ready-to-use outputs, and measurement.
4. Keep the evidence limitation and `Not scored` treatment visually prominent when the minimum evidence threshold is not met.
5. Preserve stable finding and action IDs across Markdown, HTML, discussion, and later addenda.
6. Omit irrelevant elements rather than filling the layout with generic advice.
7. Verify desktop and mobile rendering, page-level overflow, table scrolling, heading clipping, anchors, contrast, and print behaviour before delivery.

The worked example demonstrates density and hierarchy only. It is not a source of account evidence.

## Optional appendices

Include an appendix only when the user explicitly requested it or it is necessary to make an agreed action executable.

### Profile appendix

- display name;
- bio;
- banner message or visual direction;
- link destination and call to action;
- pinned-post outline.

Preserve the creator's real voice and claims. Do not manufacture credentials or proof.

### Execution appendix

Use the matching stage skill for a detailed plan. If the user explicitly requested post drafts in the same task, include the requested number with creator-specific proof requirements, natural reader actions, media jobs, and accessibility notes.

Do not automatically attach 15 fully drafted posts or a generic 30-day calendar to every report. This does not excuse abstract actions: the core report must still contain exact cleanup decisions, content types, starting briefs, and the implementation needed for A1–A3.

### Shareable summary

For a public version, include:

- one surprising observation;
- the primary constraint;
- A1–A3 in condensed form;
- one material limitation;
- no private analytics without explicit approval.

## Completion check

Before delivery, confirm that the report has:

- a stable title and audit date;
- a stated objective and evidence window;
- one stage with confidence;
- a score or an explicit reason not to score;
- one primary constraint;
- exactly three discussable action IDs;
- a cleanup register when any account or content cleanup is recommended;
- a concrete content direction and starting queue when content is part of the constraint;
- one `Start here` task plus an execution board with owner, status, deliverable, effort, and definition of done;
- ready-to-use outputs for every task the agent can complete from available evidence;
- source labels and limitations;
- a review point and stage handoff;
- no unsupported platform claims, invented evidence, mass-deletion advice, or automatic long-form execution appendix.
