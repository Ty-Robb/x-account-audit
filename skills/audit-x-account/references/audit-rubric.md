# Audit rubric

Use this rubric to keep audits comparable without implying false precision.

## Evidence requirements

Preferred evidence:

- the public profile and pinned post;
- 20–30 recent posts covering at least two weeks;
- post type and visible engagement for each post;
- analytics screenshots when the account owner supplies them;
- the intended audience and growth objective.

Minimum for a total score: profile evidence and 10 classifiable recent posts. Below that threshold, use dimension ratings without a total.

Do not substitute invented values for missing impressions or engagement.

## Confidence

- **High:** at least 20 recent posts plus visible metrics or supplied analytics.
- **Medium:** 10–19 posts, or 20+ posts with incomplete metrics.
- **Low:** fewer than 10 posts, screenshots with gaps, or substantial access limitations.

A high-confidence audit can still contain low-confidence individual inferences. Label those findings separately.

## The 100-point audit score

This is an editorial and marketing heuristic informed by published code. It is not X's internal score.

### 1. Profile conversion — 15 points

Score each item from 0 to 5:

- intended audience is immediately recognisable;
- promise or recurring value is concrete;
- proof, link, pinned post, and next action form a credible path.

Assess avatar and banner for human recognition and conversion, not as proven ranking inputs.

### 2. Topic coherence — 20 points

- **0–10:** concentration of the primary subject across the latest original and quote posts: 0 for no visible cluster, 5 for a plurality, 8 for a clear majority, 10 for a dominant cluster with varied angles.
- **0–5:** alignment between profile promise and recent posts.
- **0–5:** three or fewer recognisable, mutually reinforcing content pillars.

Use up to 15 originals and quotes as the public proxy for the code's internal recent-engaged sample. State the proxy explicitly.

### 3. Discovery inventory — 20 points

- **0–10 original/quote mix:** under 25% = 0; 25–49% = 4; 50–69% = 7; 70% or more = 10.
- **0–5 cadence:** irregular or absent = 0; repeatable but inconsistent = 3; consistent and sustainable = 5.
- **0–5 stand-alone value:** posts depend on prior context = 0; mixed = 3; most posts make sense to a new viewer = 5.

Count replies as relationship activity, not out-of-network discovery inventory. Count quote posts as discovery inventory when the creator adds substantial original context.

### 4. Predicted-action potential — 25 points

Judge the sample, not one exceptional post:

- **0–8 share utility:** distinctive evidence, tools, frameworks, references, or language worth sending to someone.
- **0–6 conversation value:** specific ideas that invite substantive replies or quotes without bait.
- **0–6 follow intent:** a clear recurring promise that makes future posts desirable.
- **0–5 attention quality:** clear openings, concrete information, and sufficient context to reward reading.

Do not translate published weights into observed reach or claim that a particular action caused distribution.

### 5. Semantic variety — 10 points

- **0–5:** varied angles, examples, evidence, and formats within the account's pillars.
- **0–5:** low repetition; no flood of near-duplicate hooks or claims.

Coherence and variety should coexist: repeat the audience promise, not the exact post.

### 6. Trust and negative-feedback risk — 10 points

- **0–5:** low risk of misleading claims, hostility, irrelevant tagging, spam, or bait-and-switch.
- **0–5:** claims are specific, attributable where needed, and appropriate for the intended audience.

Do not penalise disagreement or a distinctive voice. Penalise patterns likely to make the intended audience mute, block, report, or repeatedly dismiss the account.

## Result bands

- **85–100 — Strong foundation:** optimise proven themes and conversion.
- **70–84 — Promising:** fix one or two constraints before increasing volume.
- **55–69 — Mixed signal:** clarify the account promise and discovery inventory.
- **Below 55 — Constrained:** rebuild positioning and content architecture before chasing tactics.

Never compare scores generated from materially different evidence windows without saying so.

## Prioritisation

For each recommendation, rate:

- **Impact:** likely effect on qualified discovery or conversion.
- **Confidence:** strength of observed and code-backed evidence.
- **Effort:** time and complexity for the account owner.

Select no more than three priorities. Prefer high-impact, high-confidence changes with low or moderate effort.

## Zero-follower adaptation

When there is no post history, do not fabricate a score. Instead deliver a launch-readiness review:

1. define one audience and one recurring promise;
2. write the profile and pinned introduction;
3. choose three connected pillars;
4. prepare 15 original or substantive quote posts;
5. join relevant conversations with useful replies;
6. measure which subjects produce impressions, substantive replies, shares, follows, and profile conversion;
7. revise after the first 15 posts.

Treat published cold-start logic as a sampling opportunity, not an entitlement to reach.

## Comparison audits

Use the same evidence window and rubric for every account. Extract patterns such as positioning, topic shape, proof, format, and action utility. Do not copy phrasing, ridicule weak accounts, or infer private circumstances.
