# Stage and content playbook

Use this reference for day-zero plans, post creation, format choices, creative reviews, media specifications, and stage-specific operating plans.

This is the compact fallback bundled with `$audit-x-account`. When installed, use the dedicated stage skill for a complete operating system:

- `$launch-x-account` — Day zero;
- `$learn-x-account` — Learning;
- `$systemize-x-account` — Repeatability;
- `$compound-x-account` — Compounding.

Platform specifications checked 14 August 2026. Recheck the linked X Help pages when exact upload limits matter.

## 1. Evidence labels

Keep four kinds of guidance separate:

- **Platform specification:** a current limit or behaviour stated by X.
- **Code-backed:** a fact supported by the pinned recommendation-code snapshot.
- **Practice:** a marketing or creative heuristic that should usually help people understand or use the content.
- **Experiment:** an account-specific choice to test against its own baseline.

Never describe a preferred image size, hook structure, posting time, character count, hashtag count, or cadence as an algorithm rule unless the published source establishes it.

## 2. Select the account stage

Select the stage from the evidence state, not follower count alone. The post counts below are working thresholds, not X rules.

| Stage | Evidence state | Primary question | Default deliverable |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Day zero | No classifiable posts or performance history | Is the account ready to be understood? | Positioning, profile assets, pinned post, three pillars, first 15 posts |
| Learning | Roughly 1–30 posts without a repeatable winning pattern | Which audience, topic, and format combinations earn qualified action? | Controlled content tests and a 48-hour measurement ledger |
| Repeatability | Enough comparable posts to identify at least two patterns that repeatedly beat the account median | Can a useful pattern become a recognisable series? | Repeatable series, stronger proof, conversion path, and selective repurposing |
| Compounding | Multiple repeatable series and evidence of returning audience or reliable conversion | How can the account expand without losing coherence or trust? | Format portfolio, collaborations, deeper assets, and saturation controls |

Do not advance an account because one post went viral. Do not hold it back because its follower count is small. An established account with weak recent evidence may need the Learning plan again.

The published small-author threshold of 1,000 followers belongs to a particular cold-start scorer. It is not a universal growth-stage boundary.

## 3. Stage-specific guidance

### Day zero

Collect:

- the intended audience, recurring problem, point of view, credible proof, and account goal;
- examples, screenshots, data, stories, demonstrations, or processes the creator can publish;
- realistic production time and preferred media formats.

Prioritise:

1. a profile that states who the account is for and what recurring value it will provide;
2. a pinned introduction or flagship proof post;
3. three connected pillars rather than unrelated interests;
4. 15 stand-alone original or substantive quote posts prepared before chasing volume;
5. relevant, specific conversation participation that helps real people.

Avoid broad audience questions that require an audience to work, an empty content calendar made of announcements, and decorative media with no informational role.

### Learning

Collect each post's pillar, format, opening type, proof type, natural action, publish time, and comparable 48-hour outcome. Use medians rather than letting one outlier set the strategy.

Run a small test matrix: change one major variable at a time across topic, format, proof, or opening. Keep the audience promise stable enough to learn. Build around authentic favourites, bookmarks, substantive replies, shares, quotes, profile visits, and follows rather than raw activity.

Avoid changing niche after every weak post, copying a winner word-for-word, or using a higher cadence than the creator can make specific and useful.

### Repeatability

Turn validated patterns into named or visually recognisable series. Add stronger original evidence, examples, comparisons, demonstrations, and follow-up posts. Rework winning ideas for a new question, example, or level of depth instead of reposting near-duplicates.

Audit whether reach converts: the profile, pinned post, recurring promise, and destination should agree with what the winning posts attract.

Avoid scaling a format that earns impressions but attracts the wrong audience or produces no credible reason to follow.

### Compounding

Maintain a portfolio of proven, exploratory, and relationship content. Introduce deeper formats such as video, Articles, research, tools, events, or collaborations only where the audience job justifies the production cost.

Watch for topic dilution, creative fatigue, excessive same-author inventory, repeated conversation branches, and reliance on one distribution partner or format. Protect trust as reach expands.

Avoid treating past reach as permanent entitlement or turning every post into promotion.

## 4. Post-quality checklist

A strong post usually:

1. makes sense to a relevant stranger without missing context;
2. uses the opening to state a concrete tension, result, claim, or useful question;
3. develops one main idea rather than compressing several unrelated ideas;
4. provides evidence through an example, observation, result, source, demonstration, or clear reasoning;
5. earns a natural action: save for utility, share for relevance, reply for a real question, quote for a distinct perspective, or follow for a credible recurring promise;
6. matches the account's pillars while adding a new angle;
7. uses media only when the media carries proof, explanation, emotion, or demonstration;
8. avoids unsupported certainty, manufactured controversy, generic inspiration, and empty engagement prompts.

Do not impose a universal character length. Use the shortest form that preserves the idea and proof. Do not claim that placing a link in a reply beats placing it in the post; the examined published weights do not establish that rule.

When generating with AI, require creator-specific inputs and a final human pass. Vary sentence structure, evidence, angle, and cadence. Reject near-duplicate hooks, interchangeable filler, fake personal experience, and bulk reply templates.

## 5. Choose formats by job

| Format | Best job | Quality test | Important limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Text post | Test or express one sharp idea quickly | Is the idea specific and useful without media? | Do not make brevity a substitute for evidence |
| Single image | Show proof, a diagram, comparison, screenshot, or memorable visual | Does the image add information the text cannot convey as clearly? | Add alt text and preserve readable type on mobile |
| Multi-image post | Teach a sequence, show alternatives, or present before/after evidence | Does each panel advance the argument? | X currently accepts 1–4 photos; preview the resulting crops |
| Quote post | Add analysis, context, disagreement, or an application | Would the added text still be valuable to someone unfamiliar with the source? | A bare reaction contributes little original value |
| Reply | Build a relationship or improve a live conversation | Is it specific to the person and subject? | Treat it as relationship activity, not broad discovery inventory |
| Thread | Explain a genuinely multi-step idea | Does every post move the reader forward? | Conversation deduplication means a thread should not be treated as many independent Home candidates |
| Video | Demonstrate, narrate, compare, or show a process | Is the first frame understandable and is speech captioned? | Choose length for the idea; do not pad video to trigger a signal |
| Article | Deliver a durable deep dive that benefits from structure and embedded evidence | Is the promise strong enough to justify leaving the feed? | Publishing currently requires an eligible Premium, Premium Business, or Premium Organization account |

## 6. Images, video, and profile assets

### Official platform specifications

| Asset | Current X guidance |
| --- | --- |
| Profile photo | 400 × 400 px recommended; JPG, GIF, or PNG; animated GIFs are not supported here |
| Header/banner | 1500 × 500 px recommended; JPG, GIF, or PNG; animated GIFs are not supported here |
| Single post image | Standard aspect ratios from 2:1 through 3:4 display in full in the single-image composer |
| Post image upload | Up to 5 MB; GIF, JPEG, and PNG accepted; 1–4 photos per post |
| Animated GIF | Up to 5 MB on mobile or 15 MB on web; one GIF and not alongside multiple images |
| Non-Premium video | Up to 140 seconds and 512 MB on web |
| Premium video | Up to four hours and 16 GB, subject to the resolution rules on X's current help page |
| Web video geometry | 32 × 32 minimum; 1920 × 1200 or 1200 × 1900 maximum; aspect ratio from 1:2.39 through 2.39:1 |

Sources: [profile customisation](https://help.x.com/en/managing-your-account/how-to-customize-your-profile), [photos and GIFs](https://help.x.com/en/using-x/posting-gifs-and-pictures), and [video uploads](https://help.x.com/en/using-x/x-videos).

### Practical working templates

These are production conveniences, not ranking advantages:

- `1200 × 1200` (1:1) for a robust square post or chart;
- `1200 × 628` (about 1.91:1) for landscape artwork;
- `1080 × 1350` (4:5) for a taller single-image post within X's published full-display range;
- `1920 × 1080` (16:9) for landscape video;
- `1500 × 600` (5:2) for an Article header when the composer presents that crop; confirm the live preview because X's current Article help page does not state a fixed pixel requirement.

X's advertising specifications also use 1200 × 1200 and 1200 × 628 as recommended image sizes, but ad specifications do not prove an organic ranking benefit. See [X Ads creative specifications](https://business.x.com/en/help/campaign-setup/creative-ad-specifications-old).

Keep important text, faces, and marks away from edges because responsive crops and interface overlays vary. Check the composer preview and one mobile preview. Use high contrast and type large enough to read without opening the image.

## 7. Accessibility and creative quality

Add alt text whenever the image contains information. X allows up to 1,000 characters, but concise and objective descriptions are usually better. Capture what matters, identify screenshots or visual types when relevant, and write out or summarise important text in the image. Do not merely repeat the post copy. See X's [image-description guidance](https://help.x.com/en/using-x/write-image-descriptions).

For video, include accurate captions or meaningful on-screen text and make the opening intelligible without sound. For charts, state the conclusion in the post and describe the important trend in alt text; link to the underlying source when available.

For Articles, use a clear purpose, specific title, strong opening, short paragraphs, useful subheads, evidence, and a decisive close. X's current Article guidance recommends formatting for skimming and notes that Articles can include images, video, GIFs, posts, and links. See [About Articles](https://help.x.com/en/using-x/articles).

## 8. Stage-aware measurement

Measure creative decisions against comparable posts at the same account stage:

- **Day zero:** completion and readiness—profile clarity, proof inventory, and 15 publishable posts. Do not invent performance forecasts.
- **Learning:** median 48-hour results by pillar, format, proof, and opening; record impressions, favourites, bookmarks, substantive replies, shares, quotes, profile visits, and follows where available.
- **Repeatability:** frequency with which a series beats the account median, plus visit-to-follow or destination conversion when available.
- **Compounding:** qualified audience growth, returning engagement, series durability, collaboration quality, conversion, and negative-feedback or trust indicators.

Never compare a new format to an all-time viral outlier. Compare like with like, retain useful qualitative feedback, and decide whether to retain, revise, or stop the experiment.
