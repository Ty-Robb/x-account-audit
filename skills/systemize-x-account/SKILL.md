# Systemize Repeatable X Growth

Convert isolated winners into recognisable, sustainable series while preserving novelty and trust.

## Accept useful evidence

Prefer:

- at least 30 recent posts or another credible sample spanning multiple cycles;
- comparable 48-hour metrics by pillar, format, and series;
- owner analytics for bookmarks, profile visits, follows, and destination conversion when supplied;
- production time, existing templates, audience goal, profile, pinned post, offer, and link destination.

If the sample is smaller, proceed with lower confidence and do not manufacture a system from one outlier.

## Load the stage method

Read [references/repeatability-playbook.md](references/repeatability-playbook.md) before designing the system. Use its pattern cards, series grammar, portfolio, production loop, conversion checks, and exit criteria.

## Use persistent state when present

If `.x-growth/` exists, read `workspace.json`, `account.md`, `ledger.csv`, `experiments.md`, `series.md`, and relevant decisions before extracting patterns.

For an ongoing workspace, maintain series evidence, status, fatigue triggers, production decisions, and confirmed conversion changes. Keep recurring mechanisms separate from post wording. Re-read target files before editing and preserve user changes. The skill must also work without persistent state.

## Follow the workflow

### 1. Confirm stage fit

Require evidence of at least two audience-aligned patterns that have worked more than once. A single viral post, large follower count, or high average distorted by outliers is insufficient.

Move the account back to Learning when the apparent winners cannot be repeated or their audience quality is unknown.

### 2. Extract pattern cards

For each candidate pattern, document:

- audience problem and recurring promise;
- opening, angle, evidence, and format;
- why a reader may save, share, reply, quote, or follow;
- production cost and proof requirements;
- median outcome, hit rate, and important exceptions;
- elements that must stay recognisable and elements that must vary.

Do not confuse surface wording with the underlying mechanism.

### 3. Build series without duplication

Name the useful series and define its variation grammar: new examples, applications, comparisons, counterarguments, updates, levels of depth, or formats. Create reusable briefs, not fill-in-the-blank posts that produce near-duplicates.

Maintain a deliberate mix of proven, adjacent, and exploratory work. Set the allocation from evidence and capacity rather than a universal percentage.

### 4. Connect reach to conversion

Check whether the profile, pinned post, recurring promise, and destination match the audience attracted by the winning series. Fix the largest leak before increasing volume.

Treat profile visits and conversion as human funnel evidence, not proof of a direct ranking boost.

### 5. Build the production and review loop

Define backlog capture, evidence collection, drafting, human review, media production, publishing, 48-hour measurement, and periodic series review. Include quality gates for truth, specificity, accessibility, semantic variety, and voice.

### 6. Deliver the repeatability system

Return, in this order:

1. stage-fit finding and confidence;
2. winning pattern cards;
3. two or three named series;
4. proven/adjacent/exploratory portfolio;
5. reusable brief templates and next 15 posts;
6. profile and conversion fixes;
7. production cadence and ownership;
8. series scorecard, fatigue rules, and graduation criteria.

## Keep evidence honest

Topic coherence and semantic variety must coexist. Do not recommend repeating exact hooks, flooding one conversation, or treating a thread as several independent Home candidates. Published author-diversity and conversation-deduplication logic are constraints to interpret, not reasons to stop useful series.
